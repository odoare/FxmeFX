# FxmeTape: how to read the measurements

`tape_results.md` is generated by `tape_measure.cpp` from the FxmeTools core
code the plugin runs (see the header of that file to rebuild it). The
numbers below refer to its run of 2026-11-06.

- Small-signal response. At -40 dBFS the record and playback emphasis cancel,
  so NAB and IEC measure the same; what is left is the playback head: the
  bump (+2 dB around 45 Hz at 7.5 ips, 65 Hz at 15, 100 Hz at 30), the small
  dip above it, the roll-off under it, and the gap and spacing loss at the
  top (-1.8 dB at 15 kHz at 7.5 ips, nothing audible at 15 and 30 ips).
- Distortion. 0.3 % THD at 0 VU (-18 dBFS), 3 % about 12 dB higher, which is
  where a modern tape's maximum output level sits. It is almost all odd
  harmonics (the 3rd first): the Jiles-Atherton loop is symmetric, as tape
  with a clean bias is. There is no even harmonic content to speak of.
- Compression. The pre-emphasis makes the highs reach the tape louder, so at
  -6 dBFS a 10 kHz tone is already 3 dB down where 1 kHz has barely moved:
  tape's well-known high-frequency saturation (its saturation output level at
  10 kHz is several dB under its maximum output level at 1 kHz). NAB uses the
  same 50 us at 7.5 and 15 ips, so the two speeds compress alike; IEC at
  7.5 ips (70 us) boosts the highs more and compresses them sooner.
